Warm-Up

Probability in One Sentence

Probability = favorable outcomes / total outcomes

Every SAT probability question quietly asks, “What counts in the top, and what counts in the bottom?”

Quick Checks

  • A probability is always between 0 and 1.
  • If outcomes are equally likely, simple counting works.
  • If a question says “not,” try the complement.

A bag has 5 red marbles, 3 blue marbles, and 2 green marbles. What is the probability of drawing a blue marble?

Answer: There are 10 total marbles and 3 blue marbles, so the probability is 3/10.

Foundations

The SAT Loves Clean Counting

Single Event

Find one kind of outcome. Example: rolling a 4 on a fair die is 1/6.

Multiple Good Outcomes

Add the favorable outcomes. Example: rolling an even number is 3/6 = 1/2.

Complement

If “success” is messy, compute 1 - P(failure).

A spinner has 8 equal sections labeled 1 through 8. What is the probability of landing on a number greater than 5?

Answer: Favorable outcomes are 6, 7, and 8, so 3/8.

Emphasize equal likelihood4 / 18
Strategy Move

Use Complements for “At Least One”

Core Pattern

P(at least one success) = 1 - P(no successes)

This is often faster and less error-prone than listing several successful cases.

Typical SAT Triggers

  • At least one
  • Not all
  • More than zero

A fair coin is tossed twice. What is the probability of getting at least one head?

Solution: The only way to fail is getting no heads, or TT. So P(at least one head) = 1 - 1/4 = 3/4.

Practice 1

Probability Mini Set

1. One card is chosen from 10 cards numbered 1-10. What is the probability of choosing a prime number?

2. A die is rolled. What is the probability of not rolling a number less than 3?

3. A bag has 7 white balls and 5 black balls. What is the probability of drawing a black ball?

Answers: 1) Prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, 7, so 4/10 = 2/5. 2) “Not less than 3” means 3, 4, 5, 6, so 4/6 = 2/3. 3) 5/12.

Conditional Probability

The Denominator Changes

P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B)

Read “A given B” as: among the cases where B already happened, how many are also A?

Most Common Mistake

Students keep the original total in the denominator. Conditional probability shrinks the sample space.

Translation rule: If the question says “given that the student is in band,” the denominator becomes all students in band, not all students in the school.

Choose the Correct Denominator

Using the table on the previous slide, what is the probability that a student is in band, given that the student does not play sports?

Answers: For “given does not play sports,” use the 45 non-athletes as the denominator: 12/45 = 4/15. For “given plays sports,” use the 45 athletes: 27/45 = 3/5.

Word Problems

Turn Language into a Fraction

Of

Usually points to the numerator group you want.

Given

Usually points to the restricted denominator.

Randomly Selected

Usually means every item in that stated group is equally likely.

In a survey, 40 students own a laptop, 25 own a tablet, and 15 own both. If one of the laptop owners is selected at random, what is the probability that the student also owns a tablet?

Answer: The denominator is laptop owners, so 15/40 = 3/8.

Keep rephrasing “given” as “out of the ...”10 / 18
Data Analysis

One-Variable Data: Read the Shape First

What to Notice

  • Center: where most values cluster
  • Spread: how far values extend
  • Skew: longer tail on one side
  • Outliers: values far from the rest

SAT Shortcut

Before calculating anything, ask whether the distribution is symmetric, skewed, or affected by outliers. That tells you whether mean or median is more reliable.

Measures of Center

Mean, Median, and When They Matter

Mean

Add all values and divide by the number of values. Sensitive to outliers.

Median

The middle value once data are ordered. Resistant to outliers.

Mode

The most frequent value. Less important on the SAT, but still fair game.

For the data set 4, 5, 5, 6, 20, compare the mean and the median.

Answer: Mean = 40/5 = 8. Median = 5. The large outlier 20 pulls the mean upward.

Connect center to shape and outliers12 / 18
Measures of Spread

Range and IQR Tell Different Stories

Range

Maximum minus minimum. Uses only two values, so it can change a lot with outliers.

IQR

Interquartile range = Q3 - Q1. Focuses on the middle 50% of data and ignores extreme ends.

For 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12, 18, what are the range and IQR?

Answer: Range = 18 - 2 = 16. Median is 7, lower half is 2, 4, 5 so Q1 = 4, upper half is 9, 12, 18 so Q3 = 12, and IQR = 12 - 4 = 8.

Clarify quartiles with odd-sized data set13 / 18
Reasoning

How Shape Affects Center

Symmetric

Mean and median are usually close or equal.

Right-Skewed

Long tail to the right, so the mean is usually greater than the median.

Left-Skewed

Long tail to the left, so the mean is usually less than the median.

SAT habit: If a question asks which measure best represents a data set with an outlier, think median and IQR before mean and range.

One-Variable Data Mini Set

1. The data set is 3, 4, 4, 6, 13. Find the mean and median.

2. A data set has a strong right-skew because of one very large value. Which is more representative: mean or median?

3. Two data sets have the same mean, but Set A has a much larger range than Set B. What does that say about spread?

Answers: 1) Mean = 30/5 = 6, median = 4. 2) The median is more representative because the outlier pulls the mean. 3) Set A is more spread out.

Mixed SAT Practice

Multi-Step Problem 1

A school surveyed 120 students. Of the students surveyed, 72 take Spanish, 48 play a school sport, and 30 do both. If one of the students who takes Spanish is selected at random, what is the probability that the student also plays a school sport?

Answer: “Given that the student takes Spanish” means the denominator is 72. Since 30 students do both, the probability is 30/72 = 5/12.

Checklist: underline “given,” choose the restricted group, then place the overlap on top.

Mixed SAT Practice

Multi-Step Problem 2

The test scores in a class are 68, 70, 72, 72, 75, 76, and 98. Which measure of center best represents the data, and why?

Answer: The median best represents the data because 98 is a high outlier that raises the mean. The ordered middle score is 72.

Extension: without calculating exactly, would the mean be greater than or less than 72?

Answer: The mean would be greater than 72 because the large outlier pulls it rightward.

Wrap-Up

What to Remember on Test Day

Probability

Top is what you want. Bottom is all possible outcomes in the stated sample space.

Conditional Probability

“Given” changes the denominator. Work only inside that condition.

One-Variable Data

Describe shape first. Outliers usually mean median and IQR are safer.

Exit ticket ideas: 1) Find a conditional probability from a table. 2) Decide whether mean or median is better for a skewed set. 3) Use a complement for “at least one.”
